Difficulty Rating Rules

The rating answers one question: who can finish this route, and how long does it take them? Nothing about which tricks it uses — just that. One number per route, 1 to 10; the tier comes from the number.

The scale

  • EASY · 1–3A brand-new player finishes it.
    • 1in their first hour of playing
    • 2in their first session
    • 3in their first week
  • MEDIUM · 4–5A regular jumper finishes it.
    • 4in one sitting
    • 5over a few sessions
  • HARD · 6–8Only experienced jumpers finish it.
    • 6in one session
    • 7over a few sessions
    • 8over weeks
  • INSANE · 9–10Only the best players finish it.
    • 9a few people have
    • 10one person has, or nobody yet

Three rules

  1. Rate the route as it's meant to be played. Each route gets its own number. A skip or an exploit doesn't change it.
  2. A rating is a guess until people play it. The leaderboard tells the truth: if nobody finishes your "3", it isn't a 3. Change the number; it takes a minute and nobody minds.
  3. Not sure? Ask in #mapping. Someone who has played maps like yours will have an opinion, and that beats guessing alone. 0 means "not rated yet", which is fine while it's in testing.

Where to put it

Open your map's info asset (<LevelName>_Info, next to the level). Under Routes, one entry per finish route: the route name from your timer zones (main if there's one route) and its Number. The card shows the tier colour, the number and a meter from it.

While you're there: Thumbnail takes a square picture, 1024×1024 PNG. Take a screenshot in play, crop it square, import it next to the level.
